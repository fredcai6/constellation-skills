# Research Handoff: Institutional Learning Without Doctrine Overload

## One named question

How do safety-critical or knowledge-intensive human organizations retain lessons while preventing checklists, procedures, and doctrine from becoming too dense to use?

## Context to preserve

- Constellation learns recursively from runs, but improvements are accumulating as layered instructions.
- The emerging concern is global usability erosion: every local fix is defensible, while the resulting doctrine becomes hard to discover, interpret, and execute.
- The desired system is rigorous but personal and experimental, so distinguish universal mechanisms from heavyweight regulatory ceremony.
- The user values human judgment and wants learning systems that preserve participation rather than automate it away.

## Required comparison

Select 4-7 distinct lineages from safety-critical and knowledge-intensive organizational practice. Candidate families include aviation/checklist human factors, incident/postmortem learning, safety-management systems, lean standard work, military/emergency doctrine, medical checklist governance, and knowledge-management lifecycles.

For each distinguish:

1. How a lesson enters durable doctrine.
2. Who owns consolidation and resolves conflicts.
3. How local detail is separated from universally visible instruction.
4. What review, expiry, rehearsal, or usability testing occurs.
5. How obsolete or low-value instructions are retired.
6. How the organization detects that doctrine itself is creating risk.
7. What transfers to Constellation and what would be disproportionate.

## Constraints

- Use regulator, standards-body, institutional handbook, or primary research sources current as of 2026-07-30.
- Avoid management-blog summaries and unsourced checklist folklore.
- Do not recommend importing a full compliance regime.
- Clearly mark inference.
- Report tested and NOT tested scope.
