## Intent

_Approved by Tommy, 2026-08-03._

Phase 1 of the overhaul is done, and it left two problems behind. First, the backlog is bloated. This run started with 127 open issues, and 117 remained after the approved dispositions landed. Most of those issues are correct findings filed at the wrong granularity — noise that hides the handful of real blockers. Second, two near-term mechanisms Tommy has wanted settled keep slipping. The context governor still produces zero readings on live runs. And role instructions are still front-loaded into always-loaded prose, instead of being pushed to the agent by the spine at the gate where they matter. The spine is the gated checklist file the engine drives a run through, one gate at a time. A gate is one step in that checklist: an instruction to carry out, plus the conditions the engine checks to open the gate and to close it.

This design is the "mechanisms + cleanup" stage: make the frame hold, then clean the decks. It has five parts.

1. Fix the governor, so that every agent — subagents included — is tracked under its own identity and readings actually appear.
2. Fix the engine's output channel: the doubled instruction echo, and the anchors and constraints blocks agents cannot see. Then move the first tranche of gate-specific instructions into the spine.
3. Convert the highest-consequence prose-only invariants into wired, mechanical checks.
4. Consolidate the backlog into cluster-level items, and pay the closeout debts phase 1 left unowned.
5. Put the engine behind an MCP door, so agents drive spines through typed tools instead of the CLI. The CLI's persnicketiness has been a constant thorn — a measured share of every run's cost is fumbled flags, refusals, and help-reading. The door does two jobs: it saves those tokens in the short run, and it cleaves responsibility at a clean seam — any problem *using* the engine lands behind the MCP interface, and any problem *building contextual inputs* lands before it, so failures isolate to one side or the other instead of smearing across both. This is a deliberate first-round implementation: good-enough now over better later.

Done looks like this. The governor writes real per-agent readings on a live run, and the engine's trip mechanic acts on them. The engine's `current` output carries each instruction exactly once and renders every populated gate block. A relocated instruction demonstrably reaches agents through the engine, and the net always-loaded token count — schema cost included — gets measurably smaller. The named checks — worktree isolation (#329) and the two #328 invariants — refuse instead of hoping. The backlog is short enough that every surviving issue ties to the target architecture or to a real defect, checkable by one issue-list sweep. And an agent drives a real role spine end to end through the MCP door, with each agent holding its own server instance so work chains stay separate.

Tommy has ruled three lines of work out of this design. The vision work — the idea substrate and the federation reading of #297 — stays on the back burner. Performance-metrics methodology stays parked. The simplified-English and glossary thread is his to run elsewhere. Every threshold and every durable choice made here is graded guess/placeholder unless someone actually measured it. This is early, and we do not know what the finished project looks like.

## Chosen design

Six workstreams, ordered by dependency. A is independent. B precedes C. D and E are independent of the rest. F builds on B's output fixes and settles its caller identity alongside A. The natural cut shape is one epic with these as its issues; routing is decided at the route gate, not here.

### A. Context governor — per-agent identity (make it fire)

The governor's binding key changes from the bare `session_id` to `session_id#agentId`, using the per-subagent transcripts the harness now writes at `~/.claude/projects/<slug>/<session_id>/subagents/agent-<agentId>.jsonl`. The parent keeps its bare `session_id` key, so existing parent behavior does not change. This one re-key fixes both symptoms of #383 at once. Bindings stop piling up under a single key, so `release` can find and remove its own entry. And each key holds exactly one spine, so the gauge stops calling everything ambiguous. (A gauge is the governor's written reading of one agent's context fill: which model the agent runs, and what fraction of its context window is filled.)

The reading's consumer is the engine's own trip mechanic: at gate boundaries, a reading at or over a band restricts what the agent may do next — HARD blocks `advance` until the agent requests a refresh — forcing a handoff at a seam instead of a runaway. That consumer is live, not hypothetical: it fired twice during this exploration's own run. The done-condition is therefore not "readings appear" but "a trip fires from a per-agent reading on a live run." With workstream F, governor interaction also rides the MCP door: the advisory and the trip land in tool results, which is the primary channel for MCP-driven agents.

Interface, in deep-module terms — what a caller (the hook, the engine, a sweeper) must know:

- **Pre-build probe (precondition, recorded branch point):** inspect the real PostToolUse hook payload before any of A is built — the check is free and it selects the design. Payload carries the subagent's own transcript path: the fix is roughly a one-line re-key, and the matcher never ships. Payload carries the parent's path: lift the matcher described next. Payload carries neither shape: stop and re-scope before building anything. The probe's outcome and the branch it selected are recorded in the work area.
- **Identity resolution:** a shared identity module answers "which agent am I" by reading transcripts. It exists only in the parent-path branch above; lift it from the prototype's `agent_identity.py`: about 250 lines, with no imports from the engine or the hook. The structured matcher matches a Bash `tool_use.input.command` and never a prompt substring. A naive text match on the prompt is contaminated by construction, because dispatchers quote commands verbatim in Agent prompts. The prototype proved that live. A capability probe asserts the harness writes per-subagent transcripts (2.1.220 or later); on an older harness the governor reports which mode it is in instead of silently writing nothing.
- **Fail-closed invariant:** an unresolved identity never binds and never writes a reading. The module does not guess. Under this rule the prototype produced zero wrong records.
- **Sidechain filter inversion:** every line in a subagent's transcript is `isSidechain: true`. So the filter documented in `docs/GAUGE_WRITER_HOOK.md` must invert for subagents, and that doc's field table gains an `agentId` entry. The doc is wrong today in a load-bearing place. Fixing it belongs to this workstream, not to a follow-up.
- **Stale-binding sweep:** a one-time sweeper retires the 47 accumulated bindings unconditionally — they are pre-migration, keyed by bare `session_id`, and by construction have no per-agent transcript to test against, so no liveness test applies to them. The sweeper runs with a dry-run mode first and records the before-state, then runs for real. After its one run the script is deleted; the commit notes it existed.
- **Error modes:** transcript-format drift and the identical-command race both fail closed, so neither writes a wrong record. Identity resolution records its own duration in the gauge write, with a per-call budget (placeholder: 100ms) so a slow scan surfaces as a governor defect instead of general slowness.

Design-it-twice record: satisfied by the live variant testing in exc-6. Three identity mechanisms were compared on real dispatched subagents. The env-only mechanism could not resolve any child, and failed by design. The naive argv substring failed instructively, contaminated by verbatim dispatch. The structured field match separated cleanly and resolved all three agents. All three ran on identical input, so the recommendation is ranked by evidence, not by preference.

- **Per-section approval:** APPROVED — Tommy, 2026-08-03

### B. Engine channel fixes (precondition for C)

exc-8's cold agents found two defects in the engine's `current` output. Both must land before any relocation.

- **RAIL echo de-duplication:** the RAIL line echoes the full gate imperative word for word, so every `current` call pays for the instruction text twice. That is an exact doubling, measured at `checklist_engine.py:229`. The fix: the RAIL keeps its own rail message and points at the imperative instead of repeating it. Without this fix, moving words into the spine *increases* total token load — the relocation would cost tokens instead of saving them.
- **Render anchors and constraints:** gates carry `anchors` and `constraints` blocks that the engine never renders into `current`. Doctrine requires an agent to drive from that canonical channel, so an agent cannot see those blocks at all. 5 of 5 cold agents hit this independently. Before building the renderer, inventory what the corpus's gates actually carry in those blocks — if they turn out vestigial, deleting the two fields is the smaller fix and is on the table. Otherwise: `current` renders both blocks with the gate. This also bears on #393's finding that treatment verification proves only that a skill loaded.
- **The class, not just the instances:** the underlying contract — `current` is the complete state channel — gets a completeness property test: enumerate the fields a gate may carry and assert every populated one appears in the projection. That test catches the next field someone adds and forgets to render; a golden diff alone cannot.
- **Behavior dependency, stated:** the echo removal is assumed behavior-neutral — a graded guess, since repetition can be load-bearing for compliance. C's tracer runs against the post-B channel, so a compliance drop there indicts B before it indicts the tranche.

Design-it-twice: skipped as trivial, with the reason stated. Both changes correct how an existing projection renders, and neither adds a new interface surface. (A projection is the engine's rendered view of spine state — what `current` prints.) The behavior contract already exists: `current` is the complete state channel. These changes make the code meet it.

- **Per-section approval:** APPROVED — Tommy, 2026-08-03

### C. Selective spine-instruction relocation (first tranche: Commander execute + plan)

Move gate-specific instruction text out of the always-loaded role prose and into the spine templates the engine pushes at each gate. Do this selectively, not wholesale. The first tranche is the first batch to ship, with the rest deliberately left for later; it covers Commander's `execute` and `plan` gates, where 84% of the relocatable words concentrate. The census behind that: 54.2% of Commander's true always-loaded words are gate-specific; the overhead lives in `commander-core.md`, which is 57% gate-specific; `SKILL.md` is already 100% kernel and does not shrink further.

Interface, in deep-module terms:

- **What relocates:** the paragraph-level units that the exc-8 census classified as gate-specific. They move into the gate's imperative in `COMMANDER_SPINE.template.json`. The census method — hand classification over 97 units, reproducible with the prototype's census script — is the selection authority, with one guard: the census was a single rater's judgment, so a second independent classification pass covers the tranche units before they move, and a relocated unit must not be referenced by any other gate. Implementation makes no new judgment calls beyond that check.
- **What never relocates:** the bootstrap floor, because instantiate-and-claim text cannot ride the channel it creates; that boundary is hard and proven. Role identity, spine-use triggers, and project-focus prose also stay, per Tommy's driver 5 exceptions. Rules that span more than one gate stay. Reference-on-demand text stays behind its pointers.
- **Ordering invariant:** workstream B lands first, both fixes. Otherwise relocation doubles its own payload, and relocated constraints stay invisible.
- **Falsifiability:** each relocated unit gets two cheap checks — a render assertion (its text appears in `current` at its gate) and a named observable behavior a tracer can look for. The full exc-8 two-arm tracer then runs once at the end of the tranche, not per gate: n=3 cold runs per arm, pass means 3 of 3 instruction-arm runs perform the named behaviors and 0 of 3 no-instruction runs do (n and threshold graded placeholder). That single end-of-tranche run is also the first bulk dose-response data point.
- **Cost accounting, per run:** before the tranche ships, measure how often `current` fires per gate on a real run — the acceptance measure is total delivered tokens per run, not per call, because relocated text is paid on every `current` call at its gate while always-loaded prose is paid once. Shrink target for the tranche: `commander-core.md`'s always-loaded word count drops by at least 40% (placeholder, set against the 57% gate-specific census share).
- **Dose–response is unknown:** the live proof covers one instruction in one gate. Bulk behavior is the named untested variant — does a 10-paragraph imperative still get read and followed? The tranche is deliberately small and capped by traceability: it takes no more units than have named observables. Other roles wait for the first bulk data point. The tranche boundary is graded a guess/placeholder.

Design-it-twice record: satisfied by exc-8's testing of delivery-seam variants. Three variants were discriminated live with cold agents over two rounds: an imperative carried by the spine, always-loaded prose, and no instruction at all. The census then established where relocation pays. The choice of selective over wholesale is ranked by evidence — the census concentration, 84% on two gates.

### D. Mechanize-over-prose wiring checks (K1 first tranche)

Convert the highest-consequence prose-only invariants into wired, mechanical checks. Cluster K1 is the pattern: "built-but-not-delivered — capability ships, the wiring that makes it run does not," under umbrella issue #345. This workstream takes K1's worst instances, not the whole cluster:

- **#329:** `verify_worktree_isolation.py` exists and exits with codes, but only prose invokes it. No spine template calls it — `grep -rln` returns nothing. Meanwhile the corpus's own words call that failure "data loss, not friction." Fix: wire the script as a command-check precondition in every template whose runs enter worktrees — and because "every template" is the same omission shape that caused the defect, the fix ships with an enumeration check: a script lists the worktree-entering templates and asserts each one carries the precondition, so a template left out is a test failure rather than a silent gap. #329's own acceptance is the deliberate-breakage negative test.

This is a direct violation of the two-bin rule (doctrine B0.3), which holds that every enforced invariant must sit in one of two bins — checked by a command, or attested by a named human — because prose alone enforces nothing. Tommy's "machinize the mechanizable" ruling on #302 made this actionable.

### F. MCP front door for the engine (added mid-review from exc-9, at Tommy's direction)

The engine gains a second front door: an MCP server that exposes the drive loop as about seven tools — status, lease (claim or release), start, attest, advance, halt (block or resume), and survey result (record or consolidate). Paired verbs merge behind an `action` argument. The server wraps the engine's own dispatch function; no engine logic is duplicated, so refusals, recovery hints, rails, and the journal ride through unchanged. The CLI door stays: hooks, headless and cron runs, and non-MCP harnesses keep reaching the engine through it, and every verb the tools do not cover keeps a documented CLI fallback.

Tommy's four rulings, recorded 2026-08-03, are design constraints:

1. **Each agent gets its own server instance.** One server holds one session identity and one bound spine, so work chains stay separate. A parent and its subagent never share a lease identity, and a status call always returns the caller's own context reading — never the parent's. How a freshly dispatched subagent gets its own instance instead of inheriting the parent's is settled by ruling 4 below: the instance is keyed by the agent's identity, with per-dispatch config generation the candidate delivery mechanism, graded a guess.
2. **The schema cost is accepted.** Roughly 1,000 always-loaded tokens buy the removal of invocation strings from 7 of 21 spine-template imperatives and most of the on-demand engine reference (about 4,500 tokens), per exc-9's measurement. The net always-loaded delta across C and F together is a measured acceptance number, not a projection: on the instrumented run it must come out smaller than before. Doing better is a later item.
3. **Tool descriptions may be rich.** Teaching text inside a tool description is just-in-time delivery — the same principle as workstream C through a different channel: the harness hands the model the right sentence at the moment that verb is in play. The boundary between the two channels is one rule: a tool description teaches how to call that tool; a gate imperative carries what to do at the gate. Instruction text lives in exactly one of those homes. The planned bare-descriptions control arm is skipped by this ruling; trimming descriptions becomes a later item, chased when it is the focus.
4. **The governor lands behind the MCP too** (added at triage, 2026-08-03). Governor interaction rides tool results as the primary channel for MCP-driven agents, so the server must know which agent it serves. F therefore reuses workstream A's identity settlement — the `session_id#agentId` key — rather than inventing a second mechanism: the agent id is part of the server instance's state, and it doubles as the separation key that ruling 1 needs. One identity mechanism, two consumers.

What the door preserves, by construction: the gate imperative rides tool results verbatim, so the spine templates stay the single source of instruction text and no second rendering path exists. The governor's advisory already rides status and advance results with no new code, in the push direction doctrine requires. Project-scope `.mcp.json` ships with the repo; settings.json is never touched.

Known unknowns, graded: whether an interactive session picks up a fresh `.mcp.json` without a restart (check first at build time); behavior against a real role spine with delegation and rework (the tracer used a 4-gate toy); concurrency of two agents against separate instances. The seven-over-eighteen tool grouping is a placeholder.

Design-it-twice record: satisfied by the exc-9 live two-arm tracer — the same engine and spine driven through two competing interfaces, counted call by call (14 calls, zero fumbles through tools; 24 to 27 calls, 2 refusals, 4 to 7 help-reads through the CLI; two replicates per arm). The bare-descriptions variant that would have split structure from documentation was considered and skipped by Tommy's ruling 3, which accepts rich descriptions on their own merits.

### Threshold discipline (cross-cutting)

Every threshold, boundary, and durable choice in this spec carries a grade. Measured: the 2x RAIL echo, the census shares, and the governor variant outcomes. Guess or placeholder: the relocation tranche boundary, the K1 tranche selection, and the resolution-time budget. Placeholder grades are cheap to revise where nothing downstream hardens them; the two exceptions are stated honestly — reverting E's cluster boundaries after issues have closed means re-splitting a cluster item, and reverting C's shipped tranche means re-editing spine templates and re-running the tracer. Both are real costs, accepted. This implements Tommy's standing constraint: we do not know what the finished project looks like, and this is early.

One recorded exemption: F's bare-descriptions control arm is skipped by Tommy's ruling, so F's rich descriptions are an unmeasured preference promoted to a constraint — a deliberate first-round good-enough call in a live development stream, not a measurement.
