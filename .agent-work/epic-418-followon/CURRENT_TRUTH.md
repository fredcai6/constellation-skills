## Current planning truth

Agents drive their spines through the MCP door, and a spine is now compiled from a typed spec rather than written by hand. All eighteen engine verbs are reachable through nine tools. `generate_spine.py` refuses to write anything `validate_spine.py` would reject, calling the oracle as the literal last statement before the write, and its spec format has no raw-command field at all -- a check is a typed kind, never a shell string typed from memory.

**Four of the five definition-of-done items hold. One does not.** Agent-facing instruction still names the engine CLI in 11 shipped files: 15 fallback clauses and 9 `<engine>` tokens, with all three orchestrator spine templates among them. Two of those clauses assert that the CLI is the only path for an in-session dispatched crew, which wave 5 disproved by dispatching one. That is wave 7's R1, against issue #559.

**What the wave found is that creating the work is still three unconnected manual acts.** Nothing in this corpus provisions a worktree -- `grep -rl 'worktree add' scripts/ skills/` returns two after-the-fact verifiers and three paragraphs of prose. The worktree is typed by hand, the spine is created by a second unrelated command, and because creation is manual, close has to rediscover which worktree, which branch, and where `.agent-work/` actually landed -- a question `durable_root()` answers two different ways depending on whether an epic lease is held. The human's directive is that worktrees and spines are completely connected, and that spine closeout archives everything and reports ready to PR.

**Wave 7 is the lifecycle.** One door call takes a work id and a spec and produces the branch, the worktree, the scaffolded work area, the spine compiled into it, and the crew's environment binding -- refusing and rolling back rather than half-succeeding, and refusing outright to reuse a worktree another crew holds. The spine records where it was opened, so close reads it instead of guessing. The closing advance archives the work area with the spine file last, stages by name, commits, and reports ready to PR -- it does not open the PR, does not remove the worktree, and does not decide the work was good.

**And the dispatch itself moves into the spec.** Six sub-crews last wave were dispatched naming the wrong parent and carrying no model, against a launch order that stated both. The human's ruling settles the remedy: a template exists so a property takes effort to remove rather than effort to remember. A spec declares the crews a gate dispatches, and the generator emits a dispatch already carrying its parent and its model.

**Nonbinding forecast.** The consolidation round the human deferred, once C3 stops the tool set changing. Then wiring the lint into the corpus as a gate rather than a report, safe only once the shipped templates can pass it. Then #558 -- what a high-level review checks that a low-level review does not -- which now has five rounds of evidence behind it and needs a human ruling because it is doctrine.
