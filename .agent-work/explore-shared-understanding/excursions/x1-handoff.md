# Excursion X1 — Research handoff

**One named question:** What have Matt Pocock and the broader ecosystem actually built for (a) AI *tutoring / teaching* agents and (b) tracking a learner's *per-concept familiarity* — and what is transferable to a text-only agent context like ours (an orchestration system where agents write to a single human architect)?

**Type:** research (web + primary sources; cite everything)

**Scope — answer these, and ONLY these:**
1. Matt Pocock's tutor/teaching work specifically: what is the actual mechanism (how does it decide what to teach, at what level, and how does it track what the learner knows)? Primary sources / his own writing where possible.
2. 2–4 established prior-art patterns for modelling a learner's knowledge — e.g. knowledge tracing / learner models, spaced repetition (Leitner/SM-2), mastery learning, the "explain-back" / self-explanation effect. One-paragraph each: the mechanism, and what part transfers to *agents calibrating their register to one human* (not a classroom of students).
3. For each: what is transferable to our context and what is NOT (scoped — say what you did and did NOT evaluate).

**What "answered" looks like:** a cited findings doc, ~1 page, that a designer could read and immediately know which prior-art mechanisms are worth borrowing and which are a mismatch for a single-human text-agent setting.

**Budget / stop:** ~15 min of research; 5–8 solid cited sources is plenty. Do NOT design our solution — just report what exists and what transfers. Scoped-nulls doctrine: every "not transferable" states what was and was NOT evaluated.
