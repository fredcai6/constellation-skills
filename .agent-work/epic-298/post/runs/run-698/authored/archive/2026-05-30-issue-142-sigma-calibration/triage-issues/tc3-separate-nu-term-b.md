## Why shared ν might be limiting

Term A and term B optimize **different effective objectives**:

| Term | Gradients to σ | Role |
|------|----------------|------|
| A | Coupled with μ via retro Student-t | Mean fit + joint σ |
| B | Isolated; μ frozen | σ → \|residual\| calibration |

With **ν=4**, the Student-t log-likelihood gradient w.r.t. σ has a specific tail saturation profile. Term B's `(1+λ)×` amplifier (λ=1.0 default) doubles the σ gradient scale but **does not change tail shape**.

Hypothesis (speculative): term B may benefit from **lower ν** (heavier tails → less saturation on moderate |r/σ|) or **higher ν** (closer to Gaussian → sharper σ targeting) without perturbing term A's mean robustness.

G4 smoke showed σ learning in synthetic data with shared ν=4; no A/B ν split was tested. G5 full gold used shared ν throughout.
