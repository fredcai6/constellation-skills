## The three things worth your attention

**1. Property 2 is not enforceable on a survey.** A cold reviewer drove a generated survey to `APPROVE` with the escalation unsatisfied and nothing attached: `record()` evaluates only command-kind postconditions, `consolidate()` reads only `result`. It **BLOCKED**, and that block changed the design — the generator now refuses to inject a postcondition the engine will never consult and states the non-enforcement instead. I declined the tempting command-check fix: no artifact could honestly satisfy it, so it would have turned a gate that cannot fail into one that cannot pass — verdict B's exact defect.

**2. The settling question, answered honestly.** No, the spec no longer asks for a shell command. But the `script` probe checks flags an author *named*; it cannot see a **required** flag they *failed to name*. I proved that by authoring exactly that defect in this run's own dispatch-proof check — the generator emitted it, the oracle accepted it, and only a real dispatch found it, via a crew that blocked rather than forcing its gate. The shell-tokenization class is closed structurally; the wrong-invocation class is narrowed, not closed. Also: the generator **cannot author a TDD-shaped plan**, since a selector for an unwritten test collects zero and the probe refuses it.

**3. Three floats and six triage candidates, none filed.** The sharpest: `attach()` takes no authority and stamps `produced_by: "engine"` for every caller, so **every `user-decision` checkpoint in the corpus is satisfiable by the agent it checks** — including the four in the spine I just drove. And `recover_crews.py` misclassifies a *completed* spine-only dispatch as `NEEDS-ABANDON`, which would make a resumed Commander redispatch finished work.

I did **not** fix the four `<engine>` tokens, deliberately: they're the visible symptom of the door binding one spine at import time. Resolving them would make the instruction readable while the defect stayed — a loud defect made quiet. Recommend fixing the door first, then removing them together.
