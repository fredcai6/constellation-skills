# Excursion Brief: Deleuze's machines and agent networks

Cycle 3 (refine), `explore-memory-graph`. Human-initiated one-off.

## The one named question

Is there prior art at the intersection of Deleuze's concept of **machines** (and the surrounding assemblage/rhizome apparatus) and **agent networks / knowledge organization** — and is any of it load-bearing, or is it all decorative citation?

Context for the searcher: this project is designing a shared, cross-project agent memory backed by a knowledge graph plus a property store. The human asked whether Deleuze's machines offer anything usable for organizing *knowledge* and *agents*. We want prior art, not a fresh philosophical reading.

**Starting points to search from — do not treat as exhaustive, and do not assume all are relevant:**
- Deleuze & Guattari, *Anti-Oedipus* (1972): desiring-machines; a machine defined by connection and by *interrupting a flow* rather than by essence or function; every machine coupled to another whose flow it cuts.
- Deleuze & Guattari, *A Thousand Plateaus* (1980): **assemblage** (*agencement*), the **rhizome** and its principles (connection, heterogeneity, multiplicity, asignifying rupture, cartography, decalcomania) explicitly posed *against* arborescent/tree knowledge organization; abstract machine; lines of flight; deterritorialization/reterritorialization; **smooth vs striated space**.
- Deleuze, "Postscript on the Societies of Control" (1990): modulation, dividuals — often cited in systems/surveillance work.
- **Manuel DeLanda**, *A New Philosophy of Society* (2006) and *Assemblage Theory* (2016): the most formalized descendant — relations of exteriority, properties vs capacities, territorialization/coding parameters. Likely the best bridge to anything implementable.
- **Gilbert Simondon**, *On the Mode of Existence of Technical Objects*: individuation and concretization of technical objects; a direct influence on Deleuze and directly about machines.
- Adjacent and frequently conflated: **actor-network theory** (Latour, Callon, Law). Say clearly where ANT is doing the work that Deleuze is being credited for.
- Applied traditions worth checking: rhizomatic learning (Dave Cormier); "machinic" writing in software studies and new media; any use in multi-agent systems, self-organizing systems, ALife, or knowledge-representation literature.

## What "answered" looks like

A cited assessment with three parts:

1. **The prior art that exists** — who has actually applied these concepts to agent systems, multi-agent architectures, knowledge organization, or information retrieval. Name the work, what it claimed, and whether it produced anything operational (a system, a formalism, a design method) or stayed interpretive.
2. **A load-bearing vs decorative verdict per item.** Deleuze is cited ornamentally in tech writing very often. For each piece of prior art, judge: does the concept do work that a plainer vocabulary could not do, or is it a relabeling of something already well described (graphs, emergence, modularity, ANT, dataflow, self-organization)? Be willing to conclude "decorative" — that is a useful finding, not a failure.
3. **3–5 transferable ideas, if any**, each stated in plain operational terms with a mechanizability read: what would it change about a memory graph's structure, traversal, or agent organization? Explicitly note where an idea maps onto something the project already has (typed edges, facets, spreading activation, a read-time dial, project-owned graphs, a background crawler) — a restatement of something we have is worth knowing as such.

Also worth a line if the sources support it: whether **smooth vs striated space** maps usefully onto the project's split between free traversal and structured query, and whether the **machine-as-flow-interruption** idea says anything about an agent's read/write boundary. Do not force these — report them as null if the literature does not support them.

## Budget / stop conditions

- Budget: one focused research pass; report even if partial. Prefer a short honest answer over a long speculative one.
- Do NOT write a philosophical exegesis of Deleuze, and do NOT design anything for this project. Prior art and verdicts only.
- Do NOT inflate: if the intersection is thin, say so and say precisely what was searched.
- **Scoped nulls:** "no operational prior art found in X literature" kills that search under these conditions, never the idea that the concepts could be useful. State what was and was NOT searched.

## Research excursion

- **Sources:** web + academic literature (philosophy of technology, science and technology studies, software studies, multi-agent systems, knowledge organization, education/learning theory). Prefer primary works and peer-reviewed applications over blog-level citation.
- **Findings format:** each claim carries its source. Keep the sourced description of what someone claimed clearly separate from this excursion's own load-bearing/decorative verdict. Surface disagreement rather than smoothing it.
