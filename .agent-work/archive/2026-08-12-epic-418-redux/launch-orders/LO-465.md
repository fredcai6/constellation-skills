## Mission

Issue **#465 — "reviewer r6-fowler ships a placeholder no engine verb can fill, and filling it in
text mode rewrites every CRLF"**.

Two separate defects riding one instruction:

1. **An imperative with no affordance behind it.** `r6-fowler`'s imperative tells the reviewer to
   fill a placeholder in its own postcondition. `checklist_engine.py`'s `amend --op retext-check`
   corrects the **check text** of an engine-checked condition; it does not fill a placeholder in a
   `null`-check statement. **There is no verb for what the imperative asks.**
2. **A Windows data-corruption trap for anyone who improvises one.** The #447 g3 reviewer's first
   fill used text-mode I/O and **silently rewrote all 371 CRLF line endings** in `review.json`. It
   caught this on the diff, reverted byte-for-byte, and redid it in binary.

**Why it matters.** An instruction an agent cannot follow through the engine is an invitation to
hand-edit engine state — which the whole engine design exists to prevent (*"never hand-write or
hand-edit `spine.json`; the engine owns that state"*). The CRLF rewrite is worse than it looks: it
produces no error, passes JSON validation, and shows up as a whole-file diff that destroys blame.

## The design choice — yours, and the point of the dispatch

> Either the placeholder is removed from the shipped template, **or** an engine verb exists that
> fills it and the imperative names that verb.

Both are legitimate exits. Decide on **evidence about whether the placeholder is load-bearing**, not
on taste. Concretely: does any other reviewer gate, rail, or verifier read that postcondition's
filled value? If nothing reads it, removal is the honest fix and adding a verb is machinery for its
own sake. If something reads it, the verb is required and the imperative must name it.

Say in your return which path you took **and what evidence decided it**. A return that picks one
without having asked the load-bearing question has not done this dispatch.
