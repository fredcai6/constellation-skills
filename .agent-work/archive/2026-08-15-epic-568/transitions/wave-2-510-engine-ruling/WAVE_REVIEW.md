## Wave review — wave 2, post-repair

Two of the three refused lanes are now on `main`. The Codex tier lane needed the human's cause-based gate amendment to get there; #530 did not, and its set difference was empty in both directions — worth recording, because a gate that gets loosened should still be reported as unused when it is unused.

#510 is the interesting one. It was sent to reconcile three stale test expectations and came back having falsified the premise: two were stale, the third was a genuine contradiction between what the advisory instructs and what the engine permits. Under simulated obedience the compliance ledger brands the agent an offender for doing exactly what it was told. Neither the old nor the new wording survives that, so there was nothing to pin. The Commander stopped at its float pre-ruling instead of choosing, which is the behavior the pre-ruling existed to produce. The human ruled to change the engine rather than narrow the wording, which promotes #510 from a text repair to an engine-core implementation holding the serialized lane.

Two infrastructure findings came out of the wave rather than the code. Stale bytecode from the wave-1 worktree relocation fabricated a failure in a file byte-identical to `main`. And the MCP-only constraint written into both launch orders was unsatisfiable in every dispatched session — the door binds at import from a demo default and cannot be rebound. One Commander obeyed and lost its lease handling; the other used the engine CLI and disclosed it. That divergence, not either choice, is the finding: an impossible constraint does not stop work, it randomises it, and only the disclosing agent leaves a trace. Both are filed; the Admiral errors behind them are logged rather than smoothed over.
