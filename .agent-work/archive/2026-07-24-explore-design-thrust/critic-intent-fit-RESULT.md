# Critic findings — intent-fit lens

| ID | Lens | Severity | Finding | Disposition | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F-IF1 | intent-fit | MAJOR | The intent is billed as a dual thrust ("step-lighter, **step-back**"), but the epic that actually ships is almost entirely step-lighter. Every substantive step-back deliverable named in the Intent — architectural refactor review, test-honesty audit (Thread B), slice-wise/rolling-wave planning (Thread C) — is deferred to S11 and explicitly "NOT fully designed here." The only step-back mechanism in the immediate epic is S6. So the headline's second half is an IOU: the epic delivers ~1 of the ~4 things "step-back" is defined to mean. The title and Intent over-claim what the cut work does. | | |
| F-IF2 | intent-fit | MAJOR | S6 (guess-grading) may **shuffle** scaffolding rather than reduce it, working against the primary intent ("spend effort on the actual problem instead of scaffolding"). The schema imposes new mandatory ceremony on all planning: every decision carries an `@grade:` tag, every `guess` requires a `settle:` experiment, and pre-flight lint FAILs any ungraded decision in a recognized block. The spec asserts grading "costs one keystroke," but a `settle:` experiment is not a keystroke — it is per-guess design thought. The payoff (executor may revise guesses without reopen) is real but the net burden change is asserted, never estimated. An epic whose banner is "less scaffolding" should not add its single largest new required ritual without showing the ledger nets down. | | |
| F-IF3 | intent-fit | MAJOR | Riders that the Intent does not motivate. **S4** (launcher resolve-at-install) is a portability/correctness fix — it appears nowhere in the Intent, reduces no agent context, grades no guess, buys no information. **S10** (hardening residue: new fixture/property tests, #205 atomic meta-write bug + regression, a doc-drift comment) is engine robustness that exceeds the one test-related item the Intent actually names (S5, "the suite actually gates merges"). | F-IF4 | intent-fit | MAJOR | The spec concedes "the grand unifying theory is a fiction" and that "the unification is a viewpoint, not a premise" — which means the Intent does no load-bearing scoping work. If each piece stands on its own merit, nothing in the Intent explains why S2–S10 are one epic rather than N independent issues sequenced by ordinary priority. The risk this lens flags: the Intent reads as a post-hoc narrative banner applied over accumulated fixes. That matters because the per-section approvals and the epic framing are justified *by* the Intent; if the Intent isn't the thing that chose the scope, the approval is approving a bundle, not a purpose. | | |
| F-IF5 | intent-fit | MAJOR | S6 is designed *around* a thread the same spec defers. Its "Untaken roads" kill the `expires`/stale field as "superseded by Thread C's regenerate-don't-reread," and its named risks defer cross-artifact `leans`. | F-IF6 | intent-fit | MINOR | The Intent's magnitude premise is under-evidenced for something anchoring an epic. The claim that agents "waste effort on scaffolding" rests on x1's ~8.8k tokens/run over **6 recent transcripts**, with "archive/f1brainz not examined" (spec's own scoping note). In a 1M-context harness, 8.8k tokens/run is small in absolute terms, and the sample is self-scoped to recent runs. Whether scaffolding-reads are actually where agent effort is wasted — versus, say, planning or recovery — is not established. The Intent treats a thinly-measured symptom as the load-bearing problem. | | |
| F-IF7 | intent-fit | MINOR | Intent over-claims relative to S2's guarantee. The Intent promises agents "never need to read engine internals"; S2's INV-1 guarantees only that `current` is a superset of the arguments the caller's *next verb* needs. That leaves every other reason to read internals — debugging, understanding *why* a gate refused, inspecting cross-task or historical state — outside the guarantee. "Never" is stronger than "the next verb's args are covered." | | |
| F-IF8 | intent-fit | MINOR | The two thrusts pull opposite directions and the spec never reconciles the tension. "Step-lighter" means less ceremony; "step-back" (grade every guess, run pre-flight lint, retrospect at epic close, audit test honesty) means *more* deliberate reflective work. These can coexist only if the added reflection is precisely targeted at the real problem while ceremony is cut elsewhere — but the spec asserts the pairing as natural ("synergies taken where real") without showing that the reflection added (S6/S8) is cheaper than the scaffolding removed (S2/S9). The bundle's coherence is assumed, not argued. | | |

## Overall read

Judged only on whether the design serves its stated point, the spec is strongest exactly where the intent is narrowest — S2 (engine answerability) and S9 (return-thin) are clean, direct hits on "spend effort on the actual problem, not scaffolding." The trouble is at the edges of the bundle. The Intent advertises a co-equal "step-back" thrust that the immediate epic barely delivers (F-IF1): its substantive form is deferred to two undesigned threads, one of which (C) the shipped grading schema has already quietly made decisions against (F-IF5). Meanwhile several items ride the banner without being motivated by it (F-IF3), and the spec's own honest admission that "the grand unifying theory is a fiction" (F-IF4) removes the Intent's authority to have chosen this scope at all — leaving it reading as a narrative applied after the backlog, not the purpose that drove it. Sharpest single tension: the primary intent is "less scaffolding," yet the epic's largest new mandatory ritual is a grading-and-lint regime whose net burden reduction is asserted rather than shown (F-IF2, F-IF8). None of this says the *work* is bad — the factual anchors (no CI, real engine, real untested surfaces) all check out. It says the *framing* is doing more work than the delivery earns, and a cold reader would want the Intent rewritten to claim only what this epic ships, with step-back demoted to "seeded, not delivered."
