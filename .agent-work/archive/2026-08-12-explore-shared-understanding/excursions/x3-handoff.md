# Excursion X3 — Research handoff: the education field for teaching a working expert

**One named question:** Beyond the classroom-assessment mechanisms already surveyed (BKT, spaced repetition, mastery learning, self-explanation — see `x1-tutor-prior-art.md`), what does the education / learning-science field offer for the SPECIFIC problem of *keeping a capable practitioner current on concepts as they arise in real work* — and what transfers to a single-human, text-agent setting?

**Type:** research (web + primary/academic sources where possible; cite everything).

**Scope — cover these, ONLY these:**
1. **Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS)** — the standard architecture (domain model / student model / tutoring model / interface). What each component does, and which map onto our "concept book" (student model) and "tutor agent" (tutoring model).
2. **Scaffolding & fading; the Zone of Proximal Development** — teaching just above current ability then withdrawing support. How is "just above" estimated, and how does fading decide when to stop explaining a now-known concept?
3. **The expertise-reversal effect & the expert blind spot** — instructional support that helps novices *hurts* experts (redundancy); and experts (us) teach badly because they forget what's non-obvious. CRITICAL: our learner is an expert-in-domain who wants peer register — how should teaching differ for an expert vs a novice?
4. **Just-in-time / situated learning & cognitive apprenticeship** — learning a concept at the moment of need, in the context of real work, rather than in a curriculum. This matches "teach the concept when it comes up in the build."
5. **Concept maps / prerequisite (knowledge) graphs** — representing concepts and their dependencies; teaching in dependency order.

One paragraph each: the mechanism, and **what transfers vs. what does NOT** for a single expert learner served by text agents (scoped nulls).

**What "answered" looks like:** ~1–1.5 pages, cited, that gives a designer the field's real structure (not a YOLO guess) and a short list of transferable principles — especially how teaching an *expert* differs.

**Budget / stop:** ~15–20 min; 6–10 solid sources. Report what exists; do NOT design our solution.
