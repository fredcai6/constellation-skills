# Excursion X1 — Tutor / learner-model prior art (findings)

**Question:** What have Matt Pocock and the broader ecosystem built for (a) AI tutoring agents and (b) tracking a learner's per-concept familiarity — and what transfers to a single-human, text-only agent setting (agents writing to one "architect" who wants to stay conceptually current)?

**Method:** ~8 web sources, primary where available. Research only — no solution designed. Every "not transferable" states what was and was NOT evaluated.

---

## 1. Matt Pocock's tutor work (`/teach`) — actual mechanism

Pocock (TypeScript educator, now "AI Hero") ships a Claude Code **`/teach` skill**: "learn anything using Claude as your personalized teacher" ([aihero.dev/learn-anything-with-my-teach-skill](https://www.aihero.dev/learn-anything-with-my-teach-skill)). From his own page, the mechanism is:

- **How it decides what/at what level:** by **direct inquiry, not automatic proficiency detection.** It asks up front: *why you want to learn this, your current level, what success looks like, how you prefer to learn.* The page states "the more detail you give, the better your personalized lessons will be" — i.e. the level model is **self-reported by the human**, not inferred from performance.
- **How it tracks what the learner knows:** a persistent **"learning record"** — files under a `learning-records/` directory — so it "always knows exactly where you are in your journey." Between sessions the learner *narrates* progress ("what you learned, what was confusing, what you'd like to focus on next") and the agent **updates the record and continues building reference materials.**
- **Adaptation loop:** capture mission → find high-quality resources → generate interactive lessons (with audio + quizzes) → keep a learning record. Adaptation is **conversational + human-fed**, per-session; the page gives no algorithm for measuring comprehension gaps or auto-adjusting difficulty.
- **Companion skill `/grill-me`:** a questioning/quizzing skill — the "test the learner" half — noted across his skill set ([aihero.dev/skills](https://www.aihero.dev/skills); [shin13.github.io](https://shin13.github.io/notes/learning-from-matt-pocock-agent-skills/)).

**Scoped null:** I searched aihero.dev (`/teach` page, posts, skills index) and a third-party writeup of his skills. I did **NOT** find any published source code for `/teach`, any numeric mastery scoring, any per-concept familiarity vector, or any spaced-review scheduling. The claim "adapts to your level" is real but the *only* documented state is (a) an intake questionnaire and (b) a free-text, human-narrated learning record file. If a solution needs an automatic per-concept tracker, Pocock's public work does **not** provide one — his design deliberately **externalizes the learner model into a durable markdown record the human co-authors.** That specific choice is the most transferable idea.

**Transfers:** a persistent, human-legible "what this person knows so far" record that both sides update; asking level explicitly rather than guessing; pairing a teach mode with a `/grill-me` check.
**Does NOT transfer:** nothing here calibrates *register automatically over time* — it re-asks the human. In our setting the human is a busy architect receiving agent write-ups, not a student narrating progress each session, so a pull-the-human-for-status loop is a poor fit.

## 2. Prior-art patterns for modelling a learner's knowledge

**(a) Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) / knowledge tracing.** A hidden Markov model: each *skill/knowledge component* is a latent binary "mastered / not," updated after each observed right/wrong response using four parameters — prior, learn-rate, **guess**, **slip** — to estimate P(mastered) and predict next performance ([Wikipedia: BKT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_Knowledge_Tracing); [survey, arXiv 2105.15106](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.15106v4)). **Transfers:** the *shape* — a per-concept latent "familiarity" scalar the agent updates from evidence rather than a single global "level"; and the guess/slip intuition (one signal is noisy, update gradually). **Does NOT transfer (evaluated):** BKT needs a stream of *graded binary responses to items* per skill. Our architect produces no such answer stream, so the fitted probabilistic machinery has no observations to run on. I did NOT evaluate deep-learning KT (DKT/attention) — same missing-observations problem applies a fortiori (needs *more* interaction data).

**(b) Spaced repetition (Leitner 1972; SM-2, Woźniak 1987; FSRS 2022).** Schedule re-exposure at *expanding* intervals; each successful recall at the edge of forgetting multiplies the interval (SM-2 ease factor ~2.5), each failure resets it ([spaced-repetition guide, activerecalling.com](https://activerecalling.com/blog/spaced-repetition-ultimate-guide); [SM-2 explained, tegaru.app](https://tegaru.app/en/blog/sm2-algorithm-explained)). **Transfers:** the **decay/refresh instinct** — a concept the human hasn't touched in many sessions should be *re-surfaced or re-explained*, not assumed still-fresh; concepts touched constantly need no re-teaching. That maps cleanly onto "keep the architect current." **Does NOT transfer (evaluated):** the literal flashcard-scheduling and pass/fail-driven interval math — there is no test event and no self-rating from the architect to drive the multiplier. What transfers is the *time-since-last-contact heuristic*, not the algorithm.

**(c) Mastery learning (Bloom, 1968).** Criterion-referenced, *sequential/hierarchical*: a learner advances to new material only after demonstrating high competence (~90%) on **prerequisite** material; otherwise targeted remediation loops until mastery ([Wikipedia: Mastery learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mastery_learning); [Bloom's 2-sigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloom%27s_2_sigma_problem)). **Transfers:** the **prerequisite/dependency graph** idea — don't explain concept Y at depth to someone who lacks concept X; teach in dependency order, and gate depth on established foundations. This matches a codebase where concepts genuinely build on each other. **Does NOT transfer (evaluated):** the *gating* — you cannot block the architect from "advancing" or force a remediation loop; they set the pace and can jump anywhere. Transfers as *ordering/prioritization* advice, NOT as a hard prerequisite gate.

**(d) Self-explanation / protégé ("explain-back") effect.** Learners who *articulate their own reasoning* (or prepare to teach it) understand more deeply and, critically, produce **more accurate self-assessments** of their understanding; a partial explanation "fails in specific and informative ways, revealing exactly where understanding needs work" ([Chi self-explanation model, tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15327809jls0201_1); [protégé effect, thelearnerlab.com](https://thelearnerlab.com/protege-effect/)). **Transfers strongly:** this is the one pattern whose *observation source exists in our setting.* When the architect writes back, asks a question, or restates something, that text is a free, high-signal readout of their actual familiarity — the gaps show up in how they phrase things. An agent can calibrate register from the human's own words instead of from a quiz. **Does NOT transfer (evaluated):** the classroom framing where you *require* the learner to explain — we can't compel the architect to explain back; we can only mine whatever they already write. If they write little, this signal is thin.

## 3. Bottom line for a single-human text-agent setting

- **Best fit (observation source already exists):** the **self-explanation signal** — read familiarity off the architect's own writing — plus Pocock's **durable human-legible learning-record** (a "what they know so far" artifact both sides update).
- **Borrow the *shape*, drop the *math*:** BKT's **per-concept familiarity scalar** (not one global level) and spaced repetition's **time-since-last-contact decay** (re-surface stale concepts). Neither's update algorithm runs without graded responses; keep the intuition, not the formula.
- **Ordering not gating:** mastery learning's **dependency-ordered depth** is useful as prioritization; its hard prerequisite gate and forced remediation are a mismatch — the human is not a captive student.
- **The recurring mismatch (stated once):** every classroom mechanism assumes a stream of *graded learner responses*. Our architect emits **prose, not answers.** So the transferable core is: model familiarity **per concept**, decay it over **time/inattention**, and update it from the human's **spontaneous writing** rather than from tests. What was NOT evaluated: any Pocock `/teach` internals beyond his public pages; deep-learning knowledge tracing; and FSRS's fitted model (same no-observations barrier as SM-2).
